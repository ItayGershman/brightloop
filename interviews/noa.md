## What we've learned

- Role: PMM (product marketing manager) and content lead. Owns positioning, launch content and competitive response. Wants to know what competitors ship "before Twitter does".
- Pain, with a concrete recent example (criterion 1): strong, two cases.
  - Case A (false, last month): a Reddit thread claimed v0 was shutting down. She saw it about 18 hours after it was posted, through a Slack ping from someone on growth, and had a reaction post half-drafted before she realized it was a false rumour. Her rule since: "if it's a rumour, I don't want to see it, or at least don't label it huge."
  - Case B (real): Lovable shipped a backend with database and auth. It was posted on a Saturday night their time; she saw it on Twitter Sunday morning, about 10 to 12 hours after it went live. She spent that Sunday on a comparison post ("Brightloop does full-stack from day one, Lovable just caught up") and pushed it by Monday morning.
  - The worst part, in her words, was the noise: the same Lovable launch reached her four separate times over two days (Twitter, a newsletter on Monday, a Slack bot on Monday afternoon, a Google Alert on Tuesday), and she "wasted time figuring out it was all one thing".
  - So three pains: she hears late (10 to 18 hours), duplicates of one story arrive as separate pings, and rumours arrive looking as big as real launches.
- Current process (replies 2 and 3): Twitter, "a couple of newsletters", Google Alerts and a Slack bot. About 40 competitor alerts a week, and she ignores "nearly all of them". No fixed schedule; she skims when she can. Even the launches worth a response take her about an hour each, "because I'm starting from a blank page".
- Competitors she watches (reply 3): five buckets: Lovable, Replit, Bolt, v0, and coding agents (Codex, Claude Code).
- What makes a launch worth a response (reply 3): last month she responded to one launch (Lovable full-stack) and let two pass (a Bolt UI tweak, a Replit agent update). Her rule: respond when the move "threatened our positioning directly" and a sharp counter-narrative can go out fast. Incremental updates get no response, because "reacting would've just amplified them".
- Where the hour goes (reply 4): about half was verifying the launch was real and current, not a rumour ("the v0 thing made me paranoid"). She read Lovable's actual changelog to see what shipped versus what the Twitter thread claimed, then pulled up Brightloop's own positioning doc to find the angle. The writing itself took about 15 minutes. So the slow part is checking facts, not writing.
- When and where (reply 4): not answered. She repeated "tell me what competitors ship, I need to know before Twitter does" (word for word from reply 1) and added "If you're building something, make it work first — I just need it to work."
- Her stated want (reply 3): "three things a week, max", each with a draft post or reply already written, so she can react in five minutes. Part past behavior (one hour per response from a blank page, 40 alerts a week mostly ignored), part feature wish (the drafted post). See "Proposed for the log".
- Reachable public data for it (criterion 2): likely. The official sources for a launch (competitor blogs, changelogs, X accounts) are public, and Reddit too. Not checked by us: whether X can be read automatically within its terms or affordably; newsletters and her Slack bot aren't public web sources. A draft post would also need facts about Brightloop's own product (like "full-stack from day one"), which are not in public competitor data.
- Ends in a next action per signal (criterion 3): yes, with a clear rule. Each launch item is either "respond" (threatens Brightloop's positioning directly: write a counter-narrative post within about a day) or "let pass" (incremental: don't amplify). A rumour is held, or shown as unconfirmed.
- One job buildable by Monday (criterion 4): likely. Candidate: for the five buckets, group every mention of one launch into one item with its official source link, rank by threat to positioning, show at most three a week marked "respond", and mark the rest "let pass" or "unconfirmed" with the reason. Still open: when and where the items should reach her (she didn't say), and how often it runs.
